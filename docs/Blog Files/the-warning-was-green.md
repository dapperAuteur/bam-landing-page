# The Warning Was Green

I was importing my own bank statements into my money app. Near the top of the screen, a box said some rows needed my attention.

The box was green.

Green means good. Green means finished. The words said "stop and look," and the color said "you're done."

## People read color first

Most people do not read a screen from top to bottom. They glance. The first thing a glance picks up is color.

So when the words and the color disagree, the color often wins. Someone moving fast would read "done" and keep going.

## The rule we use now

Every message in the app gets one of three colors, and each color has one job:

- **Green**: it worked. Nothing for you to do.
- **Amber** (yellow-orange): it worked, but look at this before you move on.
- **Red**: it did not work. Something needs fixing.

Green is for success **only**. A message that asks you to do something is never green, even if most of the work went fine.

I am also making sure every message says what it means in words, not just color. Some people cannot easily tell red from green at all. Color helps people glance; words make sure everyone gets the message.

## Why this is more than taste

This was not about making the screen pretty. A green warning has real costs:

- someone skips reviewing rows that needed a decision,
- wrong data gets saved,
- they find out weeks later, when a total looks strange.

A screen's job is to get the right person to do the right thing. The wrong color quietly works against that.

## The question to ask

When a vendor demos their software, watch for one thing:

> "When something goes partly wrong, what color is the message?"

Ask them to show you a partial failure, not the happy path. Most demos only show green. You will spend your real days in the amber.

## What this means when you hire

Two questions for anyone who builds screens:

- "How do you decide what color a message is?"
- "Show me a message in your work that asks the user to do something. What does it look like?"

A good answer has rules, not feelings: "green only for success." A weak answer is "it depends on the brand colors."

## Where this stands

The fix is being built now, alongside clearer wording for credit cards: the importer will say "charge," "payment," and "refund" instead of only "income" and "expense." I will check the finished screen with my own statements before I call it done.
