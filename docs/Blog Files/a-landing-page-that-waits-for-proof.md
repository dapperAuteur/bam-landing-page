# A Landing Page That Waits for Proof

Every software company has a homepage full of benefits. Faster this. Smarter that. Some of those benefits work today. Some are on the way. From the outside, you can't tell which.

I didn't want my real estate app's homepage to work like that. So I gave it a rule, and then I made the page follow the rule by itself.

## The rule

**A benefit appears on the page only once it works on a real, live listing.**

Not "it's built." Not "it passed the tests." It works, on a house buyers can see right now.

## How the page follows it

The page checks my live listings every time someone opens it.

- **"Hide people and gear"** shows up only if a live listing actually has a photo where people were hidden.
- **"Floor plans buyers can walk"** shows up only if a live listing has a floor plan.
- **"Before and after"** shows up only if a live listing shows a room at two times.
- **"Show your seller the numbers"** shows up only once there's a live listing to count.

Today, none of those cards are on the page, even though every one of those features is built and tested. The first real house isn't live yet. When it is, the cards that it earns appear on their own.

## The sample tour does the same

The top of the page shows what a tour looks like. Until a real listing is live, it's a drawing, and it says it's a drawing.

When a real listing goes live, the drawing is replaced by the real tour of that house. The section about putting a tour on your own website does the same thing: it shows a real tour on a page and the real code to copy, not an example address.

## I took one claim off

While building this, I read every card again. One said agencies could use **their own logo**. They can't yet. There's no place to upload one. So that line came off. It goes back when the feature exists.

## One more thing it won't do

When someone on my homepage opens the sample tour, it doesn't count as a view of that house. Agents use those numbers to show their sellers how many buyers walked the house. People looking at my product page aren't buyers. I wrote about what the report counts, and what it refuses to count, in [Counts, Not Names](/blog/counts-not-names).

## The question to ask

When a vendor shows you their homepage, point at a feature and ask:

> "Can you show me this working on a real customer's page right now?"

If the answer is "it's coming soon," that's fine. But then it isn't a benefit yet. It's a plan.

## What this means when you hire

Two questions for anyone who builds or writes your marketing:

- "How would you keep our homepage from claiming more than the product does today?"
- "Which numbers would you refuse to count, and why?"

A good answer to the first describes a check, not good intentions. A good answer to the second has a reason about trust, not just accuracy.

## Where this stands

Built and tested. Right now the page shows its drawings and its basic cards. The rest arrives with the first real house.
