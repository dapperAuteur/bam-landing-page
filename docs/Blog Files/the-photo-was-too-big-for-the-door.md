# The Photo Was Too Big for the Door

Every system has a door, and every door has a size.

My real estate tours use a photo service to store the pictures and send them to buyers' phones. On my plan, that service takes photos up to 10 MB (a file size) and 25 megapixels. A megapixel is a million tiny dots. More dots, more detail.

My 360 camera makes photos of about 72 megapixels, between 20 and 40 MB each.

The photo was about three times too big for the door.

## Three ways out

1. **Pay for a bigger plan.** That works, but it costs money every month, forever, for detail nobody sees. More on that below.
2. **Ask agents to shrink each photo first,** in some other app. They would not. Or each would do it a different way, and some photos would come out blurry.
3. **Shrink it on the agent's own device,** right before it is sent, without them doing anything.

I picked the third.

## Why nobody loses anything

One fact made the choice easy. Even before this, the biggest version of a 360 photo we ever sent to a buyer was 6,144 dots wide. Their screens do not need more.

So now the app makes a copy at that same size before uploading it. The buyer sees exactly what they would have seen anyway. The extra detail was being thrown away later; now it is thrown away sooner.

iPhones get a slightly smaller copy, 4,096 dots wide. The web browser on iPhones refuses to work with pictures over a certain size, so we stay well under it.

## The fix belongs to the software

When a file is too big, someone has to deal with it. It can be the person, every single time. Or it can be the software, once.

A rule like "please keep photos under 10 MB" is the software handing its job to the person. People are busy. They will forget, guess, or give up.

## The question to ask

When you buy software that takes uploads, ask:

> "What is the biggest file your system takes, and what happens to mine if it is bigger?"

A good vendor knows the number and has a plan for bigger files. A weak one says "it should be fine."

## What this means when you hire

Two questions for anyone who builds uploads:

- "How do you find a limit before your users do?"
- "When a file is too big, whose job is it to fix: the user's, or the software's?"

A good answer names the limit and tests at it. A weak answer waits for a customer to hit it.

## Where this stands

Built and tested with a photo as large as my camera makes. The first real house will be the real test.
